The user requests deeper research and stronger marketing for Trolleys Payments and the Ledgix Digital Wallet Card. The goal is to make each service recognizable through useful situations and purposeful artwork while retaining the accepted page composition. This is a public-website design: it does not restore an Etail account application or implement provider functionality.

## Product names and scope

- **Trolleys Payments** is the full introductory name; use **Trolleys** thereafter. Describe accepting customer payments through links, invoices and QR codes, with the approved entrance at `https://trolleys.ai`. Avoid customer-facing processor, acquiring or gateway terminology.
- **Ledgix Digital Wallet Card** is the full product name. Its financial type remains a **virtual Visa secured credit card**. A deposit backs the credit account; purchases create a balance to repay separately.
- D116 records the user's confirmation that i2c supports Apple Wallet, Google Wallet and WeChat Pay for the Ledgix program. This operator-supplied confirmation is separate from public platform documentation. Cards remain **coming soon**, with applications not open and inquiries directed to `ledgix@etaildepot.com`.
- Add an eligible card to Apple Wallet or Google Wallet; link it to WeChat Pay for supported merchant payments in mainland China. Keep device, location and eligibility conditions. Do not imply universal wallets, identical setup, a shared Trolleys balance or live provisioning.

## Reference principles

- [Stripe Payment Links](https://stripe.com/payments/payment-links) and [sharing documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links/share), current pages: lead with a concrete merchant action and make the customer's next step visible. Text, email, social and QR are distribution choices for a payment request, not seven independent products. Stripe's examples describe Stripe capabilities and are not evidence of Trolleys automation, subscriptions or payout timing.
- [Stripe Checkout](https://stripe.com/payments/checkout), current page: connect a tangible purchase, its amount and the payment action. Retain Trolleys' accepted two-step illustration rather than adding a second menu or a complex simulated dashboard. Do not reproduce Stripe screenshots or attribute Stripe's performance claims to Etail.
- [American Express digital-payment presentation](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/benefits/digital-payments/), current page: organize around recognizable in-store, in-app and online situations, then explain the relevant payment option. Adapt that clarity and contextual photography; Amex rewards, security promises, issuer identity and product terms do not transfer to Ledgix.
- [Baymard homepage and navigation research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice), updated 30 September 2025: understandable imagery, clear link scope and unambiguous clickable areas help visitors find the relevant product. Applying these ecommerce findings to a two-service financial website is a design inference. Use one explicit internal detail link for each homepage product story and preserve clear active navigation; do not claim a Baymard audit or measured usability improvement.

## Trolleys stories and copy direction

| Situation | Customer-facing copy direction | Representation and boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookings and deposits | **For the next booking.** Request a deposit by text or email. Your customer reviews the amount and pays by card. | A photography session or another ordinary service booking. This is a merchant-defined advance payment; do not suggest calendar integration, automatic reservations, installment tracking or no-show protection. |
| Work and invoices | **For work well done.** Send an invoice with a way to pay by card. Keep the work, amount and payment link together. | Completed creative work or a finished service, with an invoice or email context. Do not promise automatic reminders, reconciliation, accounting integration or elimination of late payments. |
| Pop-ups and social orders | **From a conversation to a sale.** Share a payment link with an order, or display a QR code in person. Your customer pays on their own phone. | A maker's order handoff or a small market display. A social message is another place to share the link, not native marketplace integration, inventory management or guaranteed platform approval. |

The accepted explanation stays at two steps: **Create & share** — add the amount and details, then share the request; **Your customer pays** — review the details and pay by credit or debit card. A successful customer payment is not a promise of immediately available merchant funds.

The seven channel examples remain discoverable after the use cases. Use plain titles such as Text, Email, Invoice, Bill, Social, QR code and Website, with a distinct context for each. Avoid repeating the same fictional business or turning every channel into an identical checkout panel. These are illustrations, not actual customer records or provider screenshots.

## Ledgix stories and copy direction

| Situation | Customer-facing copy direction | Representation and boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily purchases | **For the everyday.** Carry your card on your phone and pay at supported checkouts. | An ordinary contactless purchase. Retain compatible-device and merchant acceptance conditions; avoid claims that a physical card or another payment method is never needed. |
| Online purchases | **For your next online purchase.** Use your virtual Visa secured credit card online. | A person making an ordinary purchase on a laptop or phone. Do not imply support at every merchant, universal browser autofill or a new single-use card number for every purchase. |
| Visiting mainland China | **For your time in China.** Link your card to WeChat Pay for purchases at eligible merchants in mainland China. | A travel purchase with a merchant QR context. Do not show a transfer, red packet, cash withdrawal or wallet top-up. This is a distinct linking flow from Apple/Google wallet provisioning. |

Keep the deposit/repayment explanation beside the account information. A phone-first presentation changes how the card is carried, not the fact that it is secured credit. Do not introduce rewards, credit-score improvement, interest rates, deposit amounts, approval speed or an issuer name without program evidence.

## Page composition and artwork

**Homepage:** preserve the accepted hero layout, shared company header/footer, typography and palette. Use a new candid order-handoff photograph without promotional overlays. The human exchange supplies context; the headline stays outside the artwork. Replace the abstract channel columns with concise booking, invoice and pop-up/social vignettes. Provide one internal detail link per product story, avoiding repeated action rows. Keep the Ledgix phone artwork and availability distinction.

**Payments:** keep the accepted two-step artwork. Add a work-focused photograph and the three practical use cases before the refreshed seven-channel presentation. If an operational summary is included, limit it to confirmed payment records, team/terminal access and service controls; do not invent workflow automation or expand the application scope.

**Cards:** keep the Digital Wallet Card phone hero. Add an everyday/online/mainland-China use section supported by photography, followed by wallet choices and the secured-credit explanation. Product-page bodies remain focused on their own service. Do not change the shared company header to a product brand.

Artwork should feel like one photographic family: believable gestures, purposeful crops, natural light, warm neutral surroundings and restrained green details. Different situations need distinct objects and settings rather than the same shop repeated. Keep customer copy in HTML, never baked into generated images. Generated people are illustrative, not named customers or endorsements; do not generate invented financial screens, readable card credentials or unofficial network marks.
