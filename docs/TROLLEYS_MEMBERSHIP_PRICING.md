## Why the processing discount must be reconsidered

D155's $49 candidate paired its monthly charge with a reduction from 3.3% to 2.9%. Reducing the subscription to $5 or $3 while retaining that 40-basis-point discount gives up more variable fee revenue than the subscription replaces above these volumes:

- $5 / 0.004 = **$1,250 collected per month**.
- $3 / 0.004 = **$750 collected per month**, comparing monthly equivalents.

These are fee-revenue crossover points against D155's proposed no-monthly-fee 3.3% base, not profitability thresholds and not a comparison with the currently published offer.

At $10,000 monthly volume, the discount is $40/month. A $5 subscription replaces only $5, reducing merchant fee billings by $35 relative to that proposed base; the annual equivalent reduces them by $37.

Using D155's hypothetical $100 average ticket, supplier cost of 2.6% + 20¢, $15 monthly support, 5¢ operations/payment and 0.05% unrecovered losses, modeled contribution is **$55** for the proposed 3.3% base; **$20** for $5 + 2.9%; and **$18** for the $3 monthly-equivalent + 2.9% option. At supplier cost 2.9% + 30¢, the latter two are **−$20 and −$22**. Acquisition, company overhead, subscription collection costs and additional membership delivery costs are excluded. These are sensitivity assumptions, not actual Infinicept prices or forecasts.

The low subscription can work alongside sustainable payment margin. It cannot be presumed to finance a broad processing discount. Infinicept's public Launchpay page describes merchant-pricing control and cost visibility; the actual program agreement determines Trolleys' costs and obligations. [Launchpay](https://www.infinicept.com/launchpay/), reviewed 22 September 2026.

## Revenue role and next decision

At 1,000 subscribers, membership produces $5,000/month on monthly billing, or $36,000/year on annual billing, equivalent to $3,000/month before billing and service costs. Annual prepayment improves cash timing; it does not turn $36 into monthly revenue. Account count, conversion and retention remain unknown.

Use membership as a small recurring software revenue stream. Successful-payment margin remains the principal economic engine under this price direction. Validate member benefit usage and incremental delivery costs before implementing the subscription; set processing only after actual supplier terms and merchant acceptance are known.

The $49 Growth candidate and its $12,250 crossover remain in D155 as historical analysis, not the current membership recommendation. Existing public prices, website, policies, database, provider configuration and deployment are unchanged.

## D157 comparison: membership, 0.1% or 10¢

22 September 2026. The user asks which model better fits the market. This is a comparison, not a selected price. Recommendation: avoid requiring a subscription for basic Create and Share; if additional margin is needed, test a successful-payment fee within the advertised total. An added 0.1 percentage point gives a candidate domestic online rate of **3.0% + 30¢**; an added 10¢ gives **2.9% + 40¢**. Neither is published or approved by this question. Clarity remains separately optional.

Stripe and Square include payment-link tools within processing offers, and Helcim lists payment tools without monthly software fees. This supports evaluating a low-commitment core offer. It does not establish willingness to pay either proposed increment or prove conversion improvement. The annual equivalent is $36 paid upfront, not a $3 monthly payment option. These compare billing methods for the same hypothetical core value; the proposed Plus membership would need its own distinct value proposition if offered alongside a transaction-based core rate.

The percentage and fixed increment are equal at a $100 ticket. Below $100 the percentage costs less; above $100 the fixed amount costs less. At $250, total fees including the current base would be $7.80 under the percentage candidate or $7.65 under the fixed candidate. A $5 monthly charge equals 0.1% of $5,000 or 50 payments at 10¢. The $3 annual equivalent equals 0.1% of $3,000 or 30 payments at 10¢. These are customer fee crossover points, not provider profit thresholds.

For a broad mix of ticket sizes, **0.1% is the first price-test hypothesis**: it scales down for small payments and creates a familiar single total rate. For the earlier proposed service segment with mostly tickets above $100, **10¢ deserves a direct comparison**, because it prices the same collection workflow consistently across larger sale values. The actual payment mix and supplier costs decide the sustainable choice. No universal market winner is established.

Do not charge per draft, link, channel, QR scan or repeated share. Bill only the defined successful-payment event. Reserve the optional $5/month or $36/year membership for verified recurring tools; do not stack it on a usage fee merely to sell the same basic Create and Share function twice. No assumed automation, unlimited messaging or vendor savings are introduced. Calculation was independently reviewed; all application and commercial settings remain unchanged.
