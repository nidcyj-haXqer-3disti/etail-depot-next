# D155 — Demand and willingness to pay for Trolleys Two Step

Research date: 22 September 2026. Research and proposal only. No application, published price, provider workflow or deployment changed. Collection automation, team work queues and reconciliation discussed below are proposed capabilities, not verified current Trolleys features. The active Etail application remains a public website.

**Recommendation:** Keep Create → Share as the simple entrance. Test a paid collection workflow for businesses chasing deposits and balances. A separate fee for creating or sharing an ordinary payment link has a weak competitive case. Demand evidence establishes a problem; it does not establish Trolleys product-market fit or willingness to pay.

## Four findings

### 1. Cash-flow pain exists alongside strong price pressure

The Federal Reserve's 2026 report, using its 2025 employer survey, reports increased operating-input costs as a challenge for 73%, paying operating expenses for 54%, and uneven cash flow for 50%. The last category includes collecting receivables; it is not a late-invoice-only measure. Reaching customers and growing sales was the most common operational problem, at 57%. [Full report, printed page 3](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms/2026-report-on-employer-firms.pdf).

The survey gathered 6,525 employer-firm responses in September–November 2025. It uses a weighted convenience sample, not random selection. Changed financial-challenge questions prevent direct year-to-year comparisons. [Report and sample](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms), [methodology](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/our-data/methodology).

**Implication:** Sell a measurable reduction in work or payment delay, then demonstrate that benefit exceeds the incremental fee. Do not present universal SMB demand, a growing trend from incomparable survey questions, or a sales-growth promise.

### 2. Payment timing is a better first segmentation variable than “all businesses”

The Fed's payments report finds processing fees especially problematic for businesses paid at purchase; businesses paid after delivery more often struggle with slow customers. Businesses paid through third parties more often report processing time and settlement/availability delays. Its payment questions cover 4,920 employer respondents from the September–November **2023** survey, published December 2024. [Federal Reserve payments findings](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/about/media/2024/payments-challenges-small-businesses-face-20241205).

**Implication:** Prioritize merchants with a request, a due date and follow-up work. Distinguish customer delay from processor settlement delay: a better request or reminder cannot promise a faster bank payout. This is older descriptive evidence, not a 2026 estimate or proof that a new workflow fixes either problem.

### 3. Late-payment research supports discovery with service firms, with sample limits

Intuit's January 2025 survey of 2,487 US businesses with 0–100 employees found 56% were owed unpaid invoices and 47% had some invoices more than 30 days overdue. The roughly $17,500 average owed is conditional on outstanding invoices; unpaid does not mean every dollar is overdue. Respondents came from a paid Dynata panel and active QuickBooks customer panel. These are vendor-sponsored, self-reported observations, with online-panel and customer-selection limitations. [Intuit report and methodology](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2025/).

**Implication:** Ask prospects to show their actual deposit/balance, reminder and matching work. These findings do not prove that Trolleys will collect faster, that the entire outstanding balance is recoverable through software, or that users will pay an extra percentage.

### 4. Generic payment links already compete as included functionality

Stripe includes Payment Links in integrated processing pricing; its page supports email/SMS sharing, QR codes and embedded buttons. Square advertises Payment Links with no monthly or upfront fee, subject to processing, and supports text, email, social, QR and website use. These are current official product offers, not independent evidence of customer outcomes. [Stripe Payment Links](https://stripe.com/payments/payment-links), [Square Payment Links](https://squareup.com/us/en/payment-links).

**Implication:** The two-step presentation can improve understanding and activation, but naming the steps is not itself a durable paid advantage. A premium must solve a distinct recurring task better than the merchant's existing processor or accounting tools. Avoid charging twice for functionality already included in the processing arrangement.

## Segment priorities to validate

The ranking below is a product hypothesis derived from the findings, not measured market share or a quantified addressable market. Goods, Services and Information describe the sale; payment timing, transaction frequency and collection effort predict the proposed workflow's fit more directly.

| Priority | Candidate | Specific job to investigate | Proposed workflow value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | Service businesses collecting a deposit and final balance | Know who owes what, when to ask, and whether someone else already followed up | Reusable requests, due-date follow-up, payment-status updates and clear team ownership |
| Second | Custom/order-based goods businesses | Match the request and payment to the right order, pickup or delivery | Order references, outstanding balances, payment/refund matching and exports |
| Focused discovery | Information sellers such as research or market-briefing businesses | Tie payment to a defined subject, delivery time, version and access scope | Versioned sale context and access/delivery references, without promising the information's outcome |
| Lower initial priority | Immediate-payment, high-volume or low-margin retail | Reduce acceptance costs and avoid changing an established checkout | Weak reason to pay an added link fee; investigate only if a separate costly collection problem exists |

Information is broader than digital downloads. Its scope, timing, freshness and promised use matter, but these sources do not measure a distinct “information payments” market or its willingness to pay. Interview that segment separately. Mixed sales can contain goods, services and information; do not force a merchant into a permanent business category.

## What could justify the proposed extra charge

Test a small operational package: a reusable request, a due date, an outstanding-payments list, configurable follow-up that stops when payment is confirmed, ownership of the next action, and matching payments/refunds to the correct request. Confirm capabilities and synchronization with the service operator before promising them. Copying a link or opening the native share sheet is the included foundation.

**Proposed +0.3%–0.5% collected-volume fee:** potentially attractive for intermittent users who prefer paying when a request succeeds. It becomes expensive for high-value transactions even if the software does the same amount of work. Test a cap or a clear monthly ceiling. Define the billed event, refunds, partial payments and exclusions before any offer; an ordinary successful payment is not evidence that software caused incremental collection.

**Proposed $19–$49 monthly plan:** potentially attractive to frequent users with repeat follow-up and team work. It provides predictable cost, but must justify paying in quiet months. Test with the same feature package as the percentage plan before concluding that either billing method is preferred.

Illustrative arithmetic, excluding processing and other services:

| Monthly volume using the proposed workflow | +0.3% | +0.5% |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $5,000 | $15 | $25 |
| $10,000 | $30 | $50 |
| $50,000 | $150 | $250 |

At 0.4%, $19 and $49 equal the variable fee at $4,750 and $12,250 of monthly volume respectively. These are comparison points, not validated optimal prices, forecasts or margins. The approved **Trolleys Clarity +0.4%** is a separate existing proposition; this research does not rename it, duplicate its charge or bundle a second 0.4% invisibly.

Measure merchant value as **labor cost saved + verified financing cost avoided + contribution from genuinely incremental completed business − extra service cost**. Do not count the full payment volume as incremental benefit when it would have arrived anyway. If quantifying earlier cash, use the merchant's actual borrowing cost and days accelerated, not the entire amount collected. Fees on large invoices need a correspondingly convincing value case.

## Willingness-to-pay validation

1. **Discovery:** Recruit merchants across the priority segments, including nonusers of Trolleys and businesses happy with existing tools. Observe a recent collection cycle and record transaction size, frequency, overdue share, follow-up time, tools, switching costs and who owns the budget. Separate customer delay, missing information and settlement delay.
2. **Usability:** Test Create → Share → follow-up/status with real tasks in a clearly identified prototype. Measure independent completion, mistakes, help required and correct understanding of what the fee covers. No production processing or capability claim is implied.
3. **Price research:** Randomize comparable prospects to transparent proposed prices of 0.3%, 0.4%, 0.5% or $19/$29/$49 monthly, holding capabilities constant. Include a credible existing-tool/no-purchase option. Ask for a choice at their actual monthly volume and an explanation, not only “would you pay?” Concept preference is weaker evidence than paid retention.
4. **Paid pilot later:** Once the workflow, fee terms and delivery arrangement are ready, seek separately authorized pilot customers. Use explicit consent and actual offers; do not charge for an unavailable capability. Compare similar cohorts or randomize at merchant level where practical. Before/after averages alone confound seasonality, ticket size and customer mix.
5. **Decision:** Continue only where users repeatedly use the workflow, demonstrate value beyond its fee and support sustainable delivery costs. If merchants like the share interface but reject the premium, include Two Step with processing and revisit the premium scope.

Track activation to first request and confirmed payment; paid renewal and usage retention; due-date payment rate and 7/30-day aging; request-to-payment timing with unpaid requests retained as censored observations; follow-up minutes; reconciliation exceptions; support load, refunds and complaints; and contribution after service delivery/support costs. Report cohort size and uncertainty. Do not call total volume, click-through rate, signup interest or a satisfaction score proof of willingness to pay.

No TAM, market-share, revenue forecast, improvement percentage or dispute-win claim is established by this research.
