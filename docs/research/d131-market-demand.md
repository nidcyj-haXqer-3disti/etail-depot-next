# D131 — market demand and customer jobs

Research date: 21 September 2026. US-first market lens for the public acquisition pages, not a conclusion about approved program geography or eligibility. These are desk-research signals. No Etail customer interviews, pricing experiment or market-size estimate was conducted.

## Segments to test, in order

### Trolleys

1. Service businesses that agree work by message/email and collect booking deposits or final invoices: studios, consultants and local service providers. Test whether creating a clear request and tracking its status removes manual work.
2. Small sellers taking occasional social, event or pop-up orders. Test whether link/QR checkout is enough; do not assume it replaces a complete point-of-sale, inventory or tax system.
3. Teams collecting for one business. Confirm the vendor's role permissions, terminal assignment and refund/dispute controls before marketing this as a primary differentiator.

Proposed job: “I have agreed the work or sale. Help me request the right amount, know whether it was paid and understand when the funds reach my bank.” The current site explains the request; the next version must explain decision costs and the money outcome.

### Ledgix

Initial research cohort: adults who are comfortable providing a security deposit and paying purchases in full, and prefer using a phone for everyday purchases. Actual consumer/business eligibility remains a program decision. Do not advertise family/minor or corporate use solely because historical local dashboards supported those concepts.

The unresolved customer objection is material: “My existing card already works in my wallet. Why should I open another account and tie up a deposit?” Candidate reasons to validate include an accessible approved eligibility model, clearer spending/repayment controls, and issuer-confirmed authorized-user capabilities. Credit-building, rewards, lower fees and no personal guarantee are not established Ledgix benefits.

## Feature priorities

| Priority | Payment journey | Card journey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Visible fees, fit/eligibility and payout conditions; two-step collection example | Visible product type, deposit/limit terms, repayment and fees; coming-soon status |
| Second | Confirmation, refund/dispute route and support; concise channel examples | Why choose the product; wallet use and support; real optional launch-update subscription |
| Third | Verified team controls and approved product evidence | Verified additional-card/authorized-user proposition and actual program evidence |
| Later | More payment methods, subscriptions or integrations only after demand and vendor verification | Rewards, financing, credit-building or international positioning only after program confirmation |

Do not select a segment on apparent market size alone. Ask about its most recent actual collection/card choice, current workaround, total cost, frequency, money timing, reasons to switch and reasons to stay. Validate interest separately from an approved application, first transaction and repeat use.
