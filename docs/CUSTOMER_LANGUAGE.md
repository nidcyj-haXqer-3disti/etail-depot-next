# Customer language

Public wording should help a merchant understand the service and the next action. Follow the existing approved page content and current user decisions. A copy cleanup must not change pricing, legal rights, eligibility or supported capabilities.

## Writing rules

Use direct, familiar wording and concise supporting text. Keep necessary qualifications and source notes readable. Avoid internal implementation terms, review status, drafting notes, artificial urgency, unsupported superlatives and repeated labels or secondary navigation layers.

Lead company positioning with merchant services for businesses and software platforms. Use credit card processing, checkout, payment methods, payouts, reconciliation, refunds and disputes where they accurately describe the page. Do not replace precise reference terminology with commercial keywords.

Use the shared Trolleys attribution and brand component where the vendor journey needs context. Sign in and Start now lead to the Welcome page, where the visitor enters their email before the next account action. Inquiry, application, account approval and activation are different events.

Card applications remain closed. Do not add an application action or imply activation. Preserve existing inquiry routes and approved public wording; do not reintroduce a closed-application notice throughout artwork merely as an implementation reminder.

For instructions, identify the actual website or app and the visible control. Keep real credentials private and use clearly fictional examples in public artwork. Do not describe a test-account walkthrough as verified live processing. Existing guide steps, warnings and estimates are changed only under their own scoped review.

Avoid dash punctuation in authored public prose. Technical identifiers, URLs, licence notices, addresses and mathematical notation retain their required characters. Preserve authentic logos and their aspect ratios.

## Review

Check headings, body text, action labels, alt text, artwork labels and metadata together. Preserve meaningful distinctions and required conditions while removing repeated or vague language. Do not claim Stripe parity, legal certification, guaranteed search ranking or measured usability from screenshots or automated checks.
