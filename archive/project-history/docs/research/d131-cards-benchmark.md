# D131 — Ledgix card decision-confidence benchmark

21 September 2026. Primary-source research for the advanced public-page proposal. This records observations and recommendations; it changes no application, provider configuration or product terms. “9/10” is a design ambition, not a measured score or a claim of equivalence with another issuer.

## Product boundary

Retain **Ledgix Digital Wallet Card** and the descriptor **virtual Visa secured credit card**. The user has confirmed a security-deposit-backed product, full monthly repayment with optional earlier/daily repayment, Apple Wallet and Google Wallet support, and coming-soon availability. The precise issuer agreement, audience/eligibility, fees, deposit amount, deposit-to-limit relationship, deposit return, credit reporting and support arrangements remain unconfirmed. The existing public site does not open accounts, collect deposits or provision cards.

The largest confidence gap is the missing offer information, not the quality of the wallet illustration. Keep the accepted design, but make the commercial decision understandable before teaching setup.

## Why choose Ledgix instead of an existing card?

Our inference: wallet support explains usage, but does not explain why someone should commit a deposit. Brex already documents wallet-compatible cards; this capability alone is not evidence of differentiation. The deposit is a customer commitment to justify, not a benefit to celebrate.

Test these positioning hypotheses rather than publish them as established advantages:

- **A clear monthly repayment commitment.** Some customers may prefer a product whose normal obligation is full monthly repayment, with earlier payments available. Test whether that rule helps their decisions enough to justify the deposit and any fees. Many existing cardholders can already choose to pay in full, so the rule alone may be insufficient.
- **An understandable route to secured credit.** If approved eligibility makes the product useful to customers underserved by alternatives, explain that route clearly. This depends on actual underwriting; neither easier approval nor a credit-building result is established.
- **Less uncertainty about the deposited money.** Transparent funding, return conditions and support could reduce anxiety. Confirm a workable provider process and test comprehension; do not imply immediate refunds or unrestricted withdrawal.

Before selecting a proposition, ask prospective users what they currently use, why that fails, what amount they would be comfortable committing, and which verified outcome would justify changing products. If building credit is their reason, reporting must be confirmed first. If no consequential advantage survives comparison, more polished wallet artwork cannot solve the offer gap.

## Naming and page structure

Keep **Cards** in navigation, **Ledgix Digital Wallet Card** as the product name, and **virtual Visa secured credit card** beside the first explanation. Describe repayment plainly. Do not introduce “prepaid,” “top up,” “cash balance,” “0% APR” or rename the legal product “charge card” without the approved issuer terminology.

Recommended page sequence:

1. **Identity and availability.** Preserve “Secured credit. Made for your wallet.”, coming-soon status, the approved card artwork and Contact Ledgix.
2. **Account in three steps.** Explain security deposit, purchases on credit and separate repayment with a small financial-flow illustration.
3. **Before you choose.** Add one compact, approved product-facts area. Use short rows with expandable detail, not another promotional card grid.
4. **Wallet setup.** Preserve the existing single Apple/Google guide. It explains a task after the visitor understands the account.
5. **Decisive questions.** Answer deposit return, payments, eligibility and help from approved program information. Avoid repeating general wallet benefits or adding another closing CTA.

The facts area should answer these questions before a future application action:

| Customer question | Required confirmation |
| --- | --- |
| Can I get this card? | Consumer/business audience, eligible locations, age/residency requirements and application/credit-check process. |
| What must I put down? | Minimum/maximum deposit, accepted method, timing, who holds it and treatment if the application or funding fails. |
| What can I spend? | Deposit-to-credit-limit rule, approval conditions, authorization holds and when repayments restore availability. |
| What does it cost? | Annual/monthly, transaction/FX, late/returned-payment and other applicable fees; interest treatment and agreement links. |
| What must I repay? | Statement cycle, full-payment due date, permitted early/daily payments, payment methods and any automatic-payment rules. |
| How do I get my deposit back? | Release/closure conditions, outstanding-balance treatment and expected return process/timing. |
| Who is responsible and who helps? | Issuer and program roles, Ledgix/operator identity, dispute/lost-device/support channels, hours and escalation. |
| Does it build credit? | Whether, to whom and what is reported. Do not market a credit-building outcome before this is confirmed. |

Keep unanswered fields in the internal offer brief. Publishing a large collection of “to be confirmed” rows would not improve customer confidence. Coming-soon inquiry copy can remain while the operator prepares the offer facts.

## Financial-flow illustration

Use two visibly separate tracks, without invented balances or a bank/provider UI:

```text
SECURITY DEPOSIT
Your funding source → Security deposit held for the credit account
                                  │
                                  └─ Backs the account under its terms

PURCHASES AND REPAYMENT
Card purchase → Pending / posted activity → Statement balance → Repayment
                                                               ↑
                                                   Your payment source
```

Show a separate credit-limit indicator associated with the card account, not a dollar-for-dollar equality to the deposit. Deposit funding must not animate into a wallet spending balance. Wallet provisioning creates a way to use the card; it does not add credit or repay debt. Repayment reduces the amount owed; restored availability depends on posting and issuer rules. A refund/adjustment should be described separately from a deposit return. The return process is a later account event, not an unrestricted Withdraw button.

Use **Security deposit**, **Credit limit**, **Available credit**, **Current balance**, **Statement balance** and **Payment due** only where the underlying definition is known. Do not combine them into “Account money.” This is an explanatory public diagram, not authorization to rebuild account software.

## Copy recommendations

Safe current explanation:

> Your security deposit backs the credit account. Purchases create a balance that you repay separately.

Candidate line reflecting the user’s intended program, to publish with confirmed issuer terms:

> Pay your statement balance in full each month. You can also make payments earlier, including daily.

This must not silently mean daily autopay, instant credit restoration, interest-free late balances or a refundable deposit on demand. Prefer **Make a payment** for repayment and **Security deposit** for collateral; do not label either “Add money.” Keep **Contact Ledgix** while applications are closed. A launch-notification action would need an actual consent, confirmation and unsubscribe flow before replacing it.

## Evidence required for the confidence target

The design can present the offer clearly; it cannot manufacture the offer. Before calling this a high-confidence launch page, obtain the approved facts above, validate the hosted application/deposit/repayment/support paths, and run moderated comprehension sessions with the confirmed target audience. Ask participants to distinguish deposit from debt, identify when full payment is due, find total costs and deposit-return conditions, and explain the next step. Record failures and revise. A numerical “9/10” assessment remains qualitative until supported by actual customer and provider evidence.
