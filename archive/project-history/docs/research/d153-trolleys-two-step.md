# D153 — Trolleys Two-Step research and presentation

Research accessed **22 September 2026**. This document records public primary-source observations and design recommendations. It does not establish Trolleys vendor configuration, trademark clearance or measured customer results.

## Recommendation

Keep the product name **Trolleys Payments**. Use **Trolleys Two-Step** as the name of its illustrated merchant experience, with the headline **Create. Share. Get paid.**

The two numbered steps are the merchant's actions:

1. **Create** — choose Goods, Services or Information, then describe the sale and its amount.
2. **Share** — choose how to present the payment link to the customer.

The buyer's subsequent checkout is an **unnumbered outcome**, clearly described as “Your customer opens the link and pays by card.” The headline describes the intended journey; it must not imply that sharing completes payment, guarantees acceptance or determines payout timing. Account approval, customer authorization, payment confirmation and settlement remain separate matters. Avoid claims such as “paid in seconds,” “accepted everywhere” or “only two clicks.”

Consolidate the existing creation artwork, sale-type gallery and channel gallery into one connected illustration. The customer should recognize one sale moving from creation to sharing, rather than encounter several independent demonstrations.

## Primary-source evidence

| Source | Observed pattern | Application to Trolleys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Stripe Payment Links overview](https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links) | Merchants create and share a link; customers use the linked payment page. Stripe distinguishes payment links from invoices directed to particular customers. | Treat sale details, sharing and checkout as related concepts. Goods / Services / Information describe what is sold, not separate payment methods. |
| [Stripe: Share a payment link](https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links/share) | The same link can be copied into email, text, social, website or app contexts. QR codes and website buttons are presentations of the underlying payment link. | Organize channels around how customers receive or discover the link. Channel growth does not require describing a new payment network. |
| [Square Payment Links](https://squareup.com/us/en/payment-links) | Basic creation uses a title, price and description. Sharing examples include email, group text, social posts, website buttons and printed QR materials. | Show a concise request with a recognizable item and amount in Create; show a concrete delivery context in Share. Do not import Square's additional features or performance claims into Trolleys. |
| [PayPal Payment Links](https://www.paypal.com/us/business/accept-payments/payment-links) | The merchant builds a payment page and shares its link or QR code; the customer then completes payment. Examples include products, services, deposits, bookings and custom orders. | Use practical sale examples while keeping buyer checkout outside the two numbered merchant actions. PayPal's supported methods and availability do not prove Trolleys support. |
| [Visa: Tap to Pay](https://usa.visa.com/pay-with-visa/contactless-payments/contactless-payments.html) | Contactless payment involves presenting a contactless card or enabled device to a compatible terminal. | QR and shared-link checkout are not Tap to Pay. Tapping a screen or copying a link does not establish NFC acceptance. |
| [Visa Click to Pay for merchants](https://www.visa.com/en-us/products/click-to-pay/merchants) | Click to Pay is a particular tokenized online-checkout capability; Visa directs merchants to their payment service providers for implementation. | Do not label the Trolleys link workflow Click to Pay or imply that its mark or capability is supported merely because Visa cards are accepted. |
| [Jimdo Payment Links](https://www.jimdo.com/payment-links/) | Its marketing uses “Create & Share” to describe payment-link setup and distribution. | The phrase is familiar descriptive language, useful as instructional copy but not evidence of a distinctive proprietary name. |
| [Hollister: Share2Pay launch](https://abercrombieandfitchcompany.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hollister-launches-share2paytm-retail-industry-first-and-new-way) | Hollister introduced Share2Pay for a shopper to share a digital shopping bag with another person who pays. | “Share to Pay” has a close existing public naming association and a different payer relationship. Prefer a clearer merchant-focused name. |

These sources establish an industry presentation pattern, not quantified market demand or evidence that a proposed name will become well known.

## Naming comparison

| Candidate | Strength | Limitation and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trolleys Two-Step** | Memorable label connecting the two merchant actions; retains the established Trolleys brand. | Pair with visible Create / Share labels so it is not mistaken for authentication or a payment-network method. Recommended experience/campaign name, not a replacement for Trolleys Payments. |
| **Create & Share** | Immediately explains the workflow in ordinary language. | Already used descriptively in the market. Use as instructional language rather than an exclusive product claim. |
| **Share to Pay** | Short and action-oriented. | Can confuse the merchant's sharing action with the buyer's payment; close to Hollister Share2Pay. Not recommended as the branded name. |
| **Trolleys Payment Links** | Recognizable category wording useful in explanatory copy. | Less memorable as a campaign. Keep the payment-link concept in supporting language rather than creating another navigation-level product. |

The lightweight public search included the exact phrase “Trolleys Two-Step,” variants of “TwoStep” with payments, “Create & Share” with payment, and “Share to Pay” / “Share2Pay.” No obvious exact Trolleys Two-Step payment product surfaced in those results. That observation is limited to the reviewed public results, **not legal clearance or a finding that the name is available**. No brand-recognition or conversion improvement is guaranteed.

## Sharing catalog

Begin with six presentations in an extensible registry:

| Presentation | Customer-facing example |
| --- | --- |
| **Copy link** | A payment link ready to paste into a suitable conversation or online location. |
| **Text** | A concise message containing the sale and payment link. |
| **Email** | A payment request with the item, amount and link. |
| **Social** | A post or direct-message context with the payment link. |
| **QR code** | A displayed code opening the same payment destination. |
| **Website** | A payment link or button within a business website. |

Copy link is a general sharing presentation, **not a new automated integration**. Additional placements can be added without hard-coding a fixed channel count. Do not imply direct sending, native platform partnerships, embedded checkout or support for a particular app without confirming those capabilities. Invoices, agreements and receipts do not return as sharing channels.

## Implementation boundaries

- This website contains a **public illustration only**. Selection changes the example; it does not create or send requests, copy a live payment URL, generate a usable payment QR code or accept payment.
- One shared purchase selection supplies the item, amount and reference across Create, Share, the checkout outcome and the existing Order Insight explanation. Channel selection changes presentation, not the sale.
- Keep the merchant's two actions visually dominant. Show the buyer checkout as a compact outcome within the same composition, with no third numbered step or competing duplicate gallery.
- Preserve the optional Dispute readiness service, the current approved processing and service prices, their fee bases and qualifications. Researching competitors does not authorize new pricing or capability claims.
- Preserve the fixed Trolleys destination, public-only architecture, coming-soon Cards service and existing data boundaries. This design work includes no vendor configuration, backend activation or deployment.
